The trick

Rules are data

Tax in one minute

Every country does the same thing,
with a different name.

When you sell something, the government says: "Add a small percentage on top, collect it from the buyer, and pass it to us."

Think of a pizza shop. The pizza costs 100. The shop adds 18 for the government. The customer pays 118, and the shop hands the 18 over later. The shop is just the collector.

The invoice app's job: calculate that extra amount correctly, label it with the local name, and print it neatly.

The big idea

Country rules are data, not code.

Think of the language menu on your phone. The apps do not change. Only the words do. We did the same for tax: one object per country, and every screen reads from it.

// utils/tax-regimes.ts (simplified)
{
  code:     'GB',
  currency: 'GBP',
  taxName:  'VAT',
  engine:   'single-rate',
  rates:    [0, 5, 20],
  taxIdLabel: 'VAT number',
  exemptNote: 'Zero-rated or reverse charge…'
}

What a regime holds

Currency and locale, tax name, allowed rates, the label for the tax ID, the list of states or regions, rounding style, fiscal year start, and the wording for exempt sales.

What screens never do

They never write "₹", "GST", "GSTIN" or "state" by hand. They ask the regime: regime.taxName, regime.taxIdLabel.

How the maths works

Eight countries, but only three engines.

split-by-region

India

Same state: tax splits in half (CGST + SGST). Different state: one IGST line.

single-rate

UK · EU · AU · SG · AE

Pick one rate from the country's list. One tax line. Simple.

manual-rate

USA · Custom

The rate depends on state and city, so you type it. Custom lets you name the tax yourself.

Adding a fourth engine is rare. Adding a country is usually just adding one object.

Selling abroad

Exports: no tax lines, one clear note.

Many countries do not charge tax when you export a service or goods. Pick a customer from another country and the app switches on the exempt treatment by itself. You can still flip it manually.

Trust

Small details that make it production-ready.

Rounding

Line by line, then add up

Tax is calculated per line item and summed, so the printed lines always add up to the total. If the tax cannot split evenly into CGST and SGST, the last part takes the odd paisa. Whole-number round-off is used only where the country rounds and the invoice is in that country's currency.

Snapshots

An invoice never rewrites history

Each invoice stores its own country, currency, exchange rate and tax treatment when you create it. Change your settings later and old invoices still print exactly as issued.

Migration

Old data upgrades itself

Early India-only records used names like gstin. On load, and on backup import, they are converted to the generic names (taxId, regionCode) so nothing is lost.

Printing

A PDF that is never blank

We copy the invoice into a clean print layer and hide the rest of the page. The old "hide everything" trick printed a blank page inside Ionic.
